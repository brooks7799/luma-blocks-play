Luma Blocks — Privacy Policy

Last updated 11 September 2026. Applies to the Luma Blocks app on iOS, Android and the web. Operated by Create Magic, LLC (hello@createmagic.co).

The short version

Luma Blocks has no account and never asks who you are. The iOS and Android versions collect crash reports and gameplay analytics, and show ads. The web version does neither — it collects no analytics and shows no ads.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, the game shows you Google's privacy choices form before any of this starts, and nothing optional is collected until you have answered it. Rejecting the optional purposes is a normal answer: the complete game still works, and you are not asked again.

Everywhere else there is no startup privacy screen. Where a US state privacy law applies, a Privacy Choices entry appears in Settings ▸ More Settings and opens the same form, so you can change your mind at any time.

We do not describe any of this data as anonymous. It is not tied to your name, but it does travel with a per-install identifier, which is a different thing.

Data that stays on your device

Your best score, unlocked themes, sound and haptic preferences and your Remove Ads entitlement are stored locally on the device (or in your browser for the web version). We cannot see them. Your privacy choices themselves are stored on the device too, by Google's consent SDK.

How your privacy choices are collected

The consent form is Google's User Messaging Platform, the same system Google requires for apps that serve ads in regulated regions. Google acts as our consent management platform: it determines from your IP address whether a form is required, shows it, records your answer on the device in the IAB Transparency & Consent Framework format, and makes that answer available to the advertising and analytics code in the app. We do not run our own location lookup and we do not use your device language, App Store country, SIM country or timezone to decide which region's rules apply to you.

Where Google reports that a privacy entry point is required for your region, Settings ▸ More Settings ▸ Privacy Choices reopens that form so you can change or withdraw your choices. Withdrawing takes effect immediately: analytics stops, and future ad requests use the new state.

Identifiers

Analytics and crash reporting use a per-install identifier rather than anything tied to you as a person: on iOS this is the vendor identifier (IDFV), and on Android it is an app-instance identifier generated at first launch. Deleting the app ends that identifier. We call this pseudonymous rather than anonymous, because data carrying a stable per-install identifier is not legally anonymous even though we do not know who you are.

The advertising identifier (IDFA on iOS, Advertising ID on Android) is used only for advertising, and only where your choices and your system settings allow it. It is resettable — and on Android deletable — in your device settings.

App Tracking Transparency (iOS)

On iOS, Apple requires a separate system permission before an app may use the advertising identifier to track you across other companies' apps and websites. Luma Blocks asks for it with Apple's standard prompt, and only when it is about to matter: at the first point in a session where the game would show an ad, not at launch. The prompt says:

Your permission helps us show more relevant ads and measure which ads support Luma Blocks. You can still play without allowing tracking.

We do not show a prompt of our own before Apple's, and we do not offer any reward for allowing tracking. Declining changes nothing about the game: you still see ads, they are simply not personalised. If you are in the EEA, UK or Switzerland and you declined the advertising purposes on the Google form, the ATT prompt is not shown at all — there would be nothing we were permitted to do with the answer. App Tracking Transparency and the European consent form are separate permissions: changing one does not change the other.

Advertising

The iOS and Android versions show interstitial ads between runs and an optional rewarded video you can choose to watch to continue a run. Ads are served by Unity LevelPlay mediation with Unity Ads as the ad network. To serve and measure ads these partners receive your IP address, device and OS information, and — only where your privacy choices and system settings allow it — the device advertising identifier. Your choices are passed to them in the standard IAB format before any ad is requested, and the ad SDK is not started at all until they have been resolved. Their policy: Unity. The web version shows no ads.

A one-time Remove Ads Forever purchase removes interstitial ads permanently. Purchases are handled entirely by Apple or Google; we receive a receipt that contains no payment details. Where analytics is permitted, the fact that a purchase happened is recorded as one event — no payment details, no receipt contents, and nothing that identifies you.

Children

Luma Blocks is a general-audience game and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal data from children. If you believe a child has provided data through the app, email us and we will delete it.

Retention and deletion

Crash reports are kept by Firebase Crashlytics for 90 days. Gameplay events are kept by Firebase Analytics for 2 months. After those periods the data is deleted or retained only in aggregate form that cannot be traced to a device.

Because the only identifier is a per-install identifier, deleting the app severs the link between you and any data already sent. To request deletion of data tied to an installation id, email us.

Your rights

Depending on where you live — including under the GDPR, the UK GDPR, the Swiss FADP and US state laws such as the CCPA/CPRA — you may have the right to access, correct, delete or restrict processing of your data, to object, to withdraw consent, to opt out of sale or sharing and of targeted advertising, and to complain to a supervisory authority.

We do not sell personal data for money. However, where your choices and system settings allow tracking, the device advertising identifier may be shared with our advertising partners in a way that some US state laws treat as a "sale" or a "share" for cross-context behavioural advertising. Where such a law applies to you, Settings ▸ More Settings ▸ Privacy Choices is the opt-out, and the choice is passed to our advertising partners in the IAB Global Privacy Platform format. You can also deny App Tracking Transparency on iOS, delete the advertising ID in Settings ▸ Privacy ▸ Ads on Android, or email us. We answer requests within 30 days.

Changes

We will update this page and the date above when anything changes, and — in the regions where consent is what we rely on — ask again through the Google form if the change is material.
